Locked, or moving.

XRP as collateral, or XRP on the cash leg.

Repo is coming to the XRP Ledger, and XRP can do two different jobs in it. Locked as collateral, the math is a consistency check in the tens to hundreds of dollars. Moving on the cash leg, the series' scenario math lands in the thousands. Which job XRP gets is Field Note 20's cargo question — asked, for the first time, by a live product.

The Living Framework Token Design Settlement Architecture November 18, 2026
Editor's note — what this is, and a sourcing caveat

Field Note No. 20 ended with a question to ask of every adoption headline: how much of the value must pass through, or sit in, the token? This month that question stopped being hypothetical. A RippleX interview at Swell described repo — the first wholesale credit market being built on the XRP Ledger — and in the example given, XRP is not the money. It is the thing locked up beside the money.

The quotes below are taken from an interview recorded at Ripple Swell in November 2026, as carried by the contemporaneous coverage of record linked in the sources — the primary video is not yet linked here, and this note will be updated when it is. Coverage published since styles Cooper's role at RippleX differently; nothing in this note depends on the title.

What was said

Jazzi Cooper of RippleX, interviewed at Swell this month, said three things that matter for this framework.

  • Repo is close. Repo transactions have been completed in a development environment, with production pilots planned for early next quarter.
  • The roles. In her words: "RLUSD is cash, XRP can be collateral."
  • The two jobs. Outside of reserves and fees, she named two main opportunities for XRP: as a bridge asset and as a collateral asset.

She did not say the development transactions used XRP. Money market fund tokens were named alongside XRP as high-quality collateral, so the first pilots may not involve XRP at all. Read through Field Note 20's map: the bridge role is the cargo job, the collateral role is the deposit job — and Ripple's own product head has just told us which one the first wholesale market on the ledger reaches for by default.

Two jobs, two kinds of number

Collateral is a stock and the cash leg is a flow. That one difference drives everything below.

XRP as collateral (the deposit job) XRP on the cash leg (the cargo job)
What it does Sits locked while someone borrows against it Sits in the middle of the payment
How long it is held The life of the loan Seconds
What sets the demand Loan balance plus a safety margin How deep the market must be for a big ticket
Who has to hold it The borrower, or someone lending XRP to the borrower Market makers
Is XRP required? No — fund tokens work too Only when the two sides hold different money
Framework gate A consistency check, formalized below Gate 1, then Gate 2
The vocabulary is Field Note 20's: collateral is the deposit job, the cash leg is the cargo job. A trade that uses XRP as collateral adds nothing to the flow through XRP — it takes XRP out of circulation instead.

The collateral check

The collateral number is not a valuation. It is a consistency check: for XRP to back a given amount of repo, the price has to be at least high enough that the posted coins, after the lender's haircut, cover the balance.

The deposit job's consistency check
price  ≥  XRP-backed repo outstanding  ÷  (1 − haircut) × XRP posted
Illustrative inputs: 30% haircut, 5 billion XRP posted. $100B backed → about $29 · $1T backed → about $286. Both inputs are placeholders, not sourced figures — labeled as such until the pilots disclose real ones.

Note what this check does not do: it does not push the price up on its own. If XRP is too cheap to back the loan book, lenders do not bid the price higher — they take a fund token instead. Collateral demand is permissive, not causal. That is the deposit job's ceiling, and it is why Field Note 20 put the two jobs in different columns.

The cash leg

On the cash leg, the series' gates set the number, at the series' published parameters — circulating supply near 63 billion and the central 1% daily turnover, with each scenario's volatility and tolerance as the calculator and terminal state them.

Gate 1 clears. The quantity identity asks whether the float can carry the flow at all. A trillion dollars a year of repo cash legs at Observatory No. 1's 20–50 modeled turns needs $20–50 billion of working float — comfortably inside any price this note discusses.

Gate 2 binds. The square-root law asks whether one large ticket can pass through without moving the price against itself. Repo tickets are wholesale-sized, and wholesale standards are tight:

Ticket · standard Daily depth needed Implied price (63B supply, 1% turnover)
$2B · institutional (10bp, σ = 3%) $1.8T ≈ $2,900
$5B · mature wholesale (10bp, σ = 2%) $2.0T ≈ $3,200
Q_max and required-price formulas, scenario volatilities and tolerance tiers exactly as published in Part I, the calculator, and the terminal. Central 1% turnover; the published range (1.5% to 0.3%) moves each figure by ÷1.5 to ×3.3. These are conditional scenario outputs, not predictions.

XRP used this way is held for seconds. The value does not come from the dollars passing through — Field Note 20's throughput-is-not-demand rule — it comes from how much XRP market makers must keep on hand so a big trade clears cleanly.

How the two jobs interact

Collateral does not compete with the cash leg. It feeds it, modestly: locked XRP leaves the float, and the same required market cap spreads over fewer coins. Five billion XRP genuinely locked takes the denominator from 63 to 58 billion and lifts each Gate 2 figure by about nine percent — roughly $2,900 to $3,100, $3,200 to $3,400. Two honest caveats. The effect is real but second-order at today's float. And it holds only if the collateral is truly locked: if the lender can re-use what is posted, the coins are back in circulation and the float has not shrunk at all.

The concession at full strength

The strongest reading against the thesis is that collateral is the only job XRP gets in repo. Here is that case with nothing held back.

  • Ripple's own product lead put a stablecoin on the cash leg. Repo is the first wholesale market being built on the ledger, and in her example XRP is not the money.
  • Collateral can be borrowed, not bought. Cooper described holders lending XRP for yield so someone else can post it. That locks float without anyone buying a single coin.
  • Nobody has to pick XRP. Money market fund tokens were named as high-quality collateral in the same breath, and they do not swing in price.
  • Collateral gets sold in a crash. When the price falls, lenders liquidate. The float comes back at the worst moment — the deposit job's demand is procyclical in reverse.
  • The competing cash leg is real. The BIS ran Project Agorá with real value in July 2026, settling across currencies with tokenised central bank reserves and bank deposits. No bridge asset was involved, and every leg was pre-funded before it moved.
  • Value capture is not automatic. Asked whether repo could capture value for XRP, Cooper said product-market fit comes first and a capture mechanism comes later. That is an honest answer, and the framework should treat it as one.

If all of that holds, repo on the XRP Ledger is good for the ledger and a modest float story for the token — a network headline, in Field Note 20's terms, with a deposit-sized token story attached.

Where the hub comes in

XRP reaches the cash leg only when the two sides of a trade hold different money. When both sides hold the same dollar token, the trade settles directly and no bridge is needed. The mismatch shows up across currencies, across issuers, and across ledgers — and Cooper made the hub case herself: pair every asset against XRP and you need a small fraction of the markets you would need pairing everything against everything, an efficiency she put at roughly fifteen-fold against stablecoin pairs. Applied to repo: ten kinds of collateral against ten kinds of cash is a hundred direct markets, or twenty through a hub. That is Field Note 20's corridor arithmetic, stated by the product's own builder.

Agorá shows the same gap from the other side. Its platform settles atomically — and every token had to be funded before it could move, with banks supplying each currency conversion from their own trading desks. A dollar stablecoin can be the hub for dollar assets, but it is fixed at a dollar: carrying trillions means trillions of them sitting ready. The pre-funding burden does not disappear in the official system; it is the open-work item in the BIS's own report.

What would have to be true

None of this is a prediction. It is sizing under stated conditions, and the conditions can be checked when the pilots start early next quarter.

For the cash-leg numbers to matter
XRP shows up on the cash side of a production trade, not only as collateral.
Trades involve more than one kind of cash or more than one currency — the mismatch a hub exists for.
Counterparties or their market makers hold their own XRP inventory rather than sourcing it just-in-time.
Tokenized bank deposits fail to solve pre-funding at scale. If they solve it, the cash-leg number falls.
For the collateral check to matter
XRP is accepted as collateral in the pilots — not only fund tokens.
Posted XRP cannot be re-used by the lender.
The amount locked is disclosed, so the float adjustment uses a real figure instead of this note's placeholder.
The pilot checklist — what to record when they start
Is XRP in the pilots at all?
Which side of the trade is it on? This is the entire question of this note.
How many kinds of cash are used?
What haircut is applied to XRP?
Does RLUSD on the ledger grow large enough to be the cash at wholesale scale?

One sentence of position, so the note cannot be read as hedging: the interview is good news for the ledger, neutral-to-good news for the token, and a live test of the only distinction this series has ever argued matters. Locked is a floor. Moving is the thesis. The pilots will tell us which one is being built.
