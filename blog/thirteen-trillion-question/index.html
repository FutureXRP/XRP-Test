The $13 trillion question.

Nobody is putting these numbers in the same room.

The SEC Chair named the problem. The private networks cannot solve it. The stablecoin float is a fraction of what is needed — and it is not even fully available for this one use case. Here is the arithmetic, and why every escape route dead-ends at the same wall.

The Living Framework Settlement Infrastructure Institutional Stack May 2026
Editor's note — framework context

This field note is not a price prediction. It is a structural argument about a gap in institutional settlement infrastructure that has become observable in public data — a gap the series has modeled theoretically since Part I but which is now visible in the volume numbers posted by Canton Network, JPMorgan Kinexys, and Broadridge's Distributed Ledger Repo platform.

The argument has two parts. First, the stablecoin float is insufficient for institutional atomic settlement — not marginally, but by an order of magnitude — and the headline figure understates the problem because the float is not available for a single use case. Second, every architectural escape route from a neutral bridge asset fails at the cross-jurisdictional corridor boundary for the same structural reason. These two findings reinforce the vacancy argument developed in Field Note 13's four-test competitive framework and Observatory No. 3's plumbing analysis.

One claim in this note carries a sourcing caveat and is stated as such: the 15–25% effective available float estimate is a reasonable industry range but is not derived from a single clean primary source. Readers should treat it as a directional bound, not a precise figure. The directional conclusion — that the effective available float is dramatically smaller than the headline number — is not in dispute.

At the 2025 SIFMA Annual Meeting, SEC Chair Paul Atkins stated the problem that the entire institutional settlement industry is circling without naming directly:

"Just imagine basically almost instantaneous DvP, RvP on chain for financial products with tokenization of securities and everything else. I think that's what really gets me very confident for the future — for de-risking, decreasing of costs."

DvP — Delivery versus Payment. RvP — Receipt versus Payment. The same atomic swap described in Field Note 13, now named explicitly by the chair of the SEC as the structural future of US capital markets. His confidence is warranted. The problem is what the numbers look like when you try to build it at the scale institutional markets actually require.

Section 01 — The numbers nobody is putting next to each other

The private institutional blockchain networks are no longer experiments. They are processing real institutional flow at volumes that would have been implausible three years ago. The Canton Network — the permissioned institutional blockchain launched by Digital Asset and backed by Goldman Sachs, BNY Mellon, and others — now reports over nine trillion dollars in monthly transaction volume. Broadridge's Distributed Ledger Repo platform, which runs on Canton, processes over four hundred billion dollars per day in repo transactions — approximately three percent of the total US repo market. JPMorgan Kinexys, the bank's institutional blockchain payment platform, is averaging five billion dollars per day and targeting ten billion.

Broadridge alone cycles a volume larger than the entire global stablecoin float every single day. This is not a rounding error. It is a structural impossibility — stablecoins cannot serve as the atomic cash leg at institutional repo scale. The arithmetic does not allow it.

Section 02 — The float problem the headline number conceals

The $320 billion figure overstates the case for stablecoins even further, because it assumes the entire supply is idle and available for a single settlement use case. It is not, and it never will be.

At any given moment, the stablecoin float is simultaneously serving multiple competing functions across global markets. Each of these functions locks supply away from any other use.

Concurrent stablecoin float demands — May 2026
Trading collateral on every centralized and decentralized exchange globally — margin, collateral pools, settlement reserves
Lending protocols and yield vaults — DeFi protocols lock billions in time-locked supply earning yield
Remittance corridors — active settlement inventory for cross-border payment flows
Dollar substitutes in dollarizing economies — Argentina, Turkey, Nigeria hold USDT as a savings instrument; this supply is not available for institutional settlement
Derivatives margin — perpetual and options platforms require posted collateral that cannot be simultaneously deployed
Cold storage and long-term holdings — a meaningful portion of total supply is simply dormant

Conservative estimates of the effective liquid, unencumbered, immediately deployable stablecoin supply available for any single settlement function at a given moment run approximately fifteen to twenty-five percent of the headline supply figure. This range is not sourced from a single primary document — it is a directional bound derived from observable on-chain behavior across DeFi protocols, exchange reserve data, and stablecoin velocity analysis. The direction is not in dispute even if the precise number is uncertain.

Effective available float for any single settlement use case
$50–80B
Against Broadridge's $400 billion per day on Canton alone. This is not a liquidity gap. It is a liquidity chasm. And it exists before you introduce a single cross-border FX corridor, a single non-dollar leg, or a single institution that is not already inside the same walled garden.

Section 03 — What is actually settling the cash leg

If stablecoins cannot serve at scale, what is settling the cash leg on Canton today? The answer is tokenized deposits — JPM Coin, LSEG DiSH instruments, bank-issued digital representations of dollars sitting in regulated bank accounts. That sounds like a solution. It is not the solution people assume it to be.

Before JPM Coin can mint, real dollars must be deposited into a JPMorgan account. That capital is immobilized. It is not earning yield elsewhere. It is not mobile across corridors. It is pre-funded — sitting in exactly the same nostro and vostro trap that has existed since correspondent banking was invented in the nineteenth century. The token moves atomically on Canton in milliseconds. The underlying dollar was trapped days earlier when the account was funded in anticipation of flow.

The atomicity is at the representation layer, not the settlement layer. Eliminating settlement risk between the tokens does not eliminate the pre-funding requirement that created the nostro/vostro problem in the first place.

For cross-border settlement, the problem compounds. JPMorgan settling a USD-to-EUR transaction atomically requires a pre-funded EUR nostro account at a European correspondent. The Canton transaction settles in seconds. The liquidity was trapped days before the chain ever saw it. This is the same Herstatt-era problem the entire tokenization movement claims to be solving — reproduced faithfully underneath a blockchain UI.

Atkins put the pre-tokenization version of this problem plainly at the December 2025 Fox Business appearance with Maria Bartiromo: every second between transaction time and clearance and settlement is risk that both parties bear. The private networks have eliminated that gap inside their systems. They have not eliminated it between their systems — or between the token and the underlying dollar that backs it.

Section 04 — The four escape routes, and why each dead-ends

Every institutional architect trying to route around a neutral bridge asset has four available options. Run each one to its logical conclusion and it terminates at the same structural failure. The failure mode differs by route. The destination is identical.

Escape route analysis — cross-jurisdictional atomic DvP at scale
Route 1 — Scale deposit tokens to all corridors
The JPM Coin approach generalized. To cover all corridors atomically, you need a JPM Coin equivalent for every currency pair that matters institutionally — MXN→PHP, NGN→IDR, BRL→KRW. That requires either one global bank with pre-funded nostro accounts in every corridor (a systemic concentration risk regulators would never permit) or a network of deposit tokens that can atomically swap cross-currency. A network of deposit tokens that atomically swap cross-currency requires a neutral intermediary between them. You have reinvented the bridge asset problem with additional counterparty risk layered on top of it.
✗ Dead end — the corridor coverage math does not close without a neutral intermediary
Route 2 — Wholesale CBDCs
The most credible institutional alternative in the literature. mBridge has BIS backing and real central bank participation from China, Hong Kong, Thailand, and the UAE. Project Agorá is working. The problems are structural rather than political: mBridge has been in pilot since 2021; wholesale CBDCs require geopolitical coordination between adversarial central banks on finality rules, failure modes, and dispute resolution; and even a successful system would cover perhaps six to eight major currency corridors. The long tail of emerging market corridors — precisely where Herstatt risk is worst and nostro pre-funding costs are highest — receives nothing. Atkins said the market needs this now. Wholesale CBDCs at settlement scale are a five-to-ten-year institutional build at minimum.
✗ Dead end — not live, not covering the corridors where the problem is most acute
Route 3 — Build a new neutral asset
A purpose-built consortium token — not XRP, something new, designed from scratch by a working group of institutions. Starting liquidity: zero. Exchange order books: do not exist. Custody infrastructure: none. Regulatory history: none. Time to build from zero to institutional-grade depth: a decade at minimum. And critically: the moment any institution issues it, it becomes that institution's liability — which reintroduces counterparty risk. The only way to make it genuinely neutral is to make it nobody's liability. That design constraint is exactly what XRP already satisfies. A working group would be building XRP from scratch, worse, slower, with more political baggage, by committee, starting from zero two-sided liquidity.
✗ Dead end — you would be building XRP from scratch at ten times the cost and time
Route 4 — Use USDC or USDT as the universal bridge
Both are widely held, deeply liquid, and present on most institutional-relevant chains. They solve the dollar leg. They do not solve the FX leg. Settling JPY→BRL atomically with USDC still requires FX conversion on both ends by a party with pre-funded accounts — the nostro problem does not disappear, it moves one step to the right. Both instruments are also USD liabilities of centralized US-regulated issuers, which means they carry the same jurisdictional exposure as JPM Coin for non-US counterparties with fiduciary mandates to avoid US legal perimeter risk. And as established in Section 02, the effective available float for any single use case is $50–80 billion — against markets requiring multiples of that daily.
✗ Dead end — cannot solve the FX leg, carries US jurisdictional exposure, arithmetic fails at scale
Every route reinvents some version of the bridge asset requirement, adds counterparty or jurisdictional risk, or requires a decade of infrastructure that does not yet exist. The structural vacancy is not a gap that better engineering of existing instruments resolves.

Section 05 — What atomic settlement requires at the network boundary

Canton and Kinexys have built excellent atomic settlement inside their walls. JPM Coin settles atomically between JPMorgan clients on Canton. LSEG DiSH tokenized deposits have executed as the cash leg of cross-border intraday repo transactions on Canton in the sterling corridor. USDC completed live US Treasury financing on Canton in August 2025. These are genuine achievements. The domestic US corridor between two US institutions settling US Treasuries through DTCC's Canton infrastructure works. It is real. This note does not dispute it.

What neither system solves is the boundary — where two networks, two currencies, or two institutions that do not share a common permissioned ledger need to settle against each other. That boundary is where Herstatt risk lives. It is where Kinexys's own documentation estimates $914 billion in settlement failures have accumulated over the past decade. It is where the $13 trillion daily repo market eventually hits a wall as it attempts to go 24/7, cross-border, and cross-currency.

Solving that boundary requires an asset that satisfies four conditions simultaneously. This is the same four-test framework introduced in Field Note 13's competitive landscape section — applied here to the cash leg question at cross-jurisdictional scale.

A deposit token fails jurisdictional neutrality — it is an issued liability of a specific bank inside a specific legal perimeter. A stablecoin fails the same test and also cannot be acquired and liquidated at the required scale within the settlement window. A wholesale CBDC passes neutrality in theory but does not exist at operational scale and covers only major currency corridors. A new consortium token fails on liquidity — it does not exist, and building liquidity from zero requires a decade.

XRP passes three of four tests. The conditional on the second test — acquire and liquidate within the settlement window — is the exact question the series' pricing analysis addresses: at what market depth can a $500 million to $2 billion atomic DvP transaction execute within institutional slippage tolerance? That is not a question about whether XRP is structurally the right instrument. It is a question about whether the market has yet priced in the depth required to make it operationally viable at that ticket size. At $1.35, the series' answer is no. At the prices the framework derives, the answer changes.

Section 06 — The adoption path that is not complicated

The institutional complexity argument against XRP was real in 2018. In 2026, each component of that argument has either been resolved or substantially addressed.

Integration: Ripple Prime holds NSCC and FICC membership following the SEC case resolution. The Hidden Road acquisition connects directly into prime brokerage infrastructure, giving institutional counterparties a regulated entry point to XRPL settlement that did not exist two years ago. Institutional custody through Anchorage Digital and BitGo is live and operational.

Regulatory standing: The SEC case is resolved — XRP is not a security in secondary market transactions. NYDFS BitLicense is held. RLUSD operates under a New York trust charter and is already paired with XRP liquidity on XRPL, giving institutions a bank-grade stablecoin native to the same rails — the dollar corridor covered by a regulated instrument while the cross-currency corridor is covered by the bridge asset.

Liquidity: Sufficient for current ODL corridor volumes. The series' framework shows that liquidity scales with institutional adoption — this is how every settlement network in history has grown. CLS did not have sufficient liquidity before the first FX settlement. SWIFT did not have sufficient liquidity before the first interbank message. Liquidity is attracted by volume, not a precondition for it.

The adoption path requires exactly one decision: a single Tier 1 bank determines that the nostro pre-funding cost on a high-volume non-dollar corridor exceeds the operational complexity of XRPL integration. They run a pilot. Settlement arrives faster. Counterparties observe zero failed trades. The switching cost advantage accumulates. Network effects begin. The rest follow — because the alternative is being the last institution still pre-funding nostro accounts in a world that has moved on. This is how SWIFT grew. It is how CLS grew. It is how every settlement network in history has scaled from first mover to standard infrastructure.

What to hold from this note

The structural argument, summarized
The private networks have solved the domestic corridor. Canton + JPM Coin + LSEG DiSH is a genuine, operational solution for same-jurisdiction, same-currency-bloc institutional settlement. Credit is due. The series does not dispute it.
The stablecoin float cannot serve as the atomic cash leg at institutional repo scale. The total supply is $320 billion. The effective available float for any single use case is $50–80 billion at best. Broadridge processes more than that every single day on Canton alone. The arithmetic is not close.
Deposit tokens reproduce the nostro/vostro problem one layer down. The token moves atomically. The underlying dollar was pre-funded days earlier. The pre-positioning requirement — the capital immobilized in anticipation of flow — is the same trap correspondent banking created. Blockchain rails do not eliminate it; they obscure it.
Every architectural escape route terminates at the bridge asset requirement. Four routes. Four dead ends. Each for a different structural reason. The vacancy is not solvable with better versions of existing instruments.
The adoption path is not complicated once the first mover decision is made. The infrastructure exists. The regulatory standing exists. The liquidity gap is a function of price, not architecture. That is precisely what the series' pricing framework models: at what price does the market have sufficient depth to execute the transactions that require it?
Until the system operates at institutional scale, the framework holds. The constraint is real. The outcome is conditional.
